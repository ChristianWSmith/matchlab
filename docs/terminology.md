# Scientific Terminology Audit

Canonical vocabulary for the matchlab project. All terms are defined in the context of this simulation framework and may differ from informal usage elsewhere.

---

## Core Entities

**Player**
A synthetic or real participant in the matchmaking ecosystem. In matchlab, every player is represented by a `PlayerReality` (ground truth the simulation knows) and a `PlayerObservation` (what algorithms see). Players are identified by a `PlayerId`.

**Agent**
A programmatic entity that controls a player's behavior. Agents can be ordinary (follow standard matchmaking rules) or adversarial (boosters, derankers, AFK players). Adversarial agents implement the `AdversarialAgent` trait and act according to a defined objective.

**Party**
A group of players who queue together and must be placed on the same team. Solo players are parties of size 1. Parties are tracked by `PartyRegistry` and enforced by `PartyIntegrityConstraint`.

**Team**
A group of players assigned to one side of a match. Teams are defined by `TeamComposition` (which specifies `team_size_a` and `team_size_b`, plus optional roles). In a standard 5v5 match, Team A and Team B each have 5 players.

---

## Experimental Design

**Condition**
A specific combination of factor levels in an experiment. In a study with two rating systems and two matchmakers, there are four conditions (cells). Each condition is run as one or more independent replications.

**Factor**
An independent variable that is systematically varied across conditions. For example, `rating.system` and `matchmaking.script` are factors in a feedback-loop study.

**Level**
A specific value assigned to a factor. For example, `elo` and `glicko2` are levels of the `rating.system` factor.

**Replication**
A single repeat of a condition with a different random seed. Replications quantify random variation and enable statistical inference. The number of replications per condition determines statistical power.

**Run**
One execution of a simulation loop for a single condition and seed. A replication may consist of one run (independent strategy) or multiple runs sharing random streams (CRN strategy).

**Study**
A structured comparison of two or more conditions, defined by a YAML manifest. Studies specify arms (conditions), replication count, seed strategy, and analysis configuration. Results are aggregated across replications to estimate treatment effects.

**Experiment**
A single simulation run or a set of runs governed by one `ExperimentConfig`. Experiments produce an `ExperimentResult` containing metrics, metadata, and a config hash for reproducibility. A study is a multi-experiment structure.

---

## Measurement

**Observation**
The data that an algorithm or system is permitted to see about a player. Defined by `PlayerObservation`: rating, rating deviation, games played, win rate, queue time, region, role. Observations never include ground truth (skill, improvement rate). The separation between observation and reality is a core design invariant.

**Metric**
A quantitative summary of simulation behavior, computed from match results and world state. Metrics are implemented as `MetricCollector` instances (Lua scripts). Examples: `rating_accuracy`, `queue_time`, `match_quality`, `convergence`. Each metric produces a `MetricResult` (scalar, distribution, summary, histogram, or time series).

**Estimand**
The population-level quantity that a statistical analysis aims to estimate. Matchlab defines estimands as `AbsoluteDifference`, `RelativeDifference`, `PercentImprovement`, `MeanPairedDifference`, and `MedianPairedDifference` between conditions on a given metric.

---

## Causal Inference

**Treatment**
The condition that receives the intervention being studied. In an Elo-vs-Glicko comparison, whichever rating system is the "new" or "challenger" is the treatment.

**Control**
The baseline condition against which the treatment is compared. Typically the status quo or reference implementation.

---

## Algorithms & Systems

**Policy**
A rule or set of rules that governs matchmaking, detection, or intervention decisions. Policies include hard constraints (must be satisfied) and soft objectives (weighted optimization). Examples: `MatchPolicy`, `AdaptivePolicy`.

**Strategy**
A plan of action adopted by an adversarial agent or an experimental design. Adversarial strategies include `RatingDumpStrategy`, `QueueGamingStrategy`, and `WinTrade`. Experimental strategies include `independent`, `crn`, and `counterfactual`.

---

## Player Modeling

**Population**
The full set of synthetic players in a simulation. Generated by `PopulationGenerator` from `PopulationConfig`, which specifies archetype proportions, total size, and skill distributions. Populations are static (generated once at t=0) unless dynamic skill is enabled.

**Archetype**
A named category of players with shared statistical properties: skill distribution, improvement rate, play frequency, session length, quit probability, and optional role. Archetypes define the composition of a population. Example: "casual" (low skill variance, low play frequency) vs "hardcore" (high skill variance, high play frequency).

---

## Skill & Performance

**Latent Skill**
The true, unobservable ability of a player, stored in `PlayerReality.skill` as a `SkillVector`. Never exposed to rating or matchmaking algorithms. The outcome model reads it to decide match results. Skill may be static or dynamic (advancing over time via `SkillProcess`).

**Performance**
The realized expression of skill in a single match, after noise is applied. Performance is computed by a `PerformanceModel` and stored in `PlayerPerformance`. Two players with identical skill will have different performances due to random variation.

**Outcome**
The result of a match: winner, team compositions, scores, per-player performances, and duration. Generated by the outcome model's `simulate` function from latent skill, not from ratings.

---

## Rating Systems

**Rating**
A visible numerical estimate of a player's skill, stored in `PlayerObservation.rating`. Computed by a `RatingSystem` (e.g., Elo, Glicko-2, TrueSkill) after each match. Initialized from population archetype settings or a fixed starting value.

**Rating Deviation (RD)**
A measure of uncertainty in a player's rating. Higher RD means the rating is less reliable (few games played or inconsistent results). Used by Glicko-2 and TrueSkill; set to a fixed 350 for Elo. Stored in `PlayerObservation.rating_deviation`.

**Volatility**
The degree of expected variation in a player's performance from game to game. Used by Glicko-2 to adjust RD growth. Stored in `PlayerObservation.volatility`.

---

## Matchmaking

**Match Quality**
A score in [0, 1] measuring how balanced a proposed match is, computed as `1 - (|avg_a - avg_b| / 400).clamped(0, 1)` where `avg_a` and `avg_b` are the average ratings of the two teams. A quality of 1.0 means perfectly balanced teams; 0.0 means teams are 400+ points apart.

**Queue Time**
The duration a player waits in the queue before being placed in a match. Measured as `now - joined_at` (in seconds). The queue time metric records the wait at match formation time, not match end.

---

## Truth & Representation

**Ground Truth**
The complete, objective state of the simulation that algorithms are not permitted to see. Includes `PlayerReality` (true skill, improvement rate, games played) and the simulation's internal state. The outcome model reads ground truth to decide match results. Metrics also read ground truth to evaluate algorithm performance.

**Observation** (see above)
The algorithm-visible subset of player state. The separation between ground truth and observation is the "truth separation" principle: no rating system, matchmaker, or detection system may access `PlayerReality` directly.

---

## Statistical Inference

**Confidence Interval (CI)**
A range of values [lower, upper] that is expected to contain the true parameter with probability `conf` (typically 0.95). Matchlab supports bootstrap, paired bootstrap, Student's t, and Welch's t methods. CIs are reported on effect sizes and per-arm statistics.

**Effect Size**
A standardized measure of the magnitude of a difference between conditions. Matchlab reports Cohen's d (mean difference pooled standard deviation), relative difference, and percent improvement. Convention: d = 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.

**p-value**
The probability of observing an effect as extreme as (or more extreme than) the measured effect, assuming the null hypothesis (no difference) is true. Matchlab computes p-values via bootstrap resampling or Welch's t approximation. Not interpreted as a binary significance flag; always reported alongside CIs and effect sizes.

---

## Power & Sample Size

**Power**
The probability of detecting a true effect of a given size, given the sample size and significance level. Matchlab computes achieved power from the observed effect size and replication count, and plans required replications from a target power (typically 0.8).

**Sample Size**
The number of independent observations (replications) per condition. Determines the precision of effect estimates and the power of statistical tests.

---

## Dynamic Behavior

**Convergence**
The process by which ratings approach true skill over time. Measured by `rating_accuracy` (MAE between rating and true skill) and `convergence` (number of games to reach within X% of final accuracy). A system converges if MAE decreases monotonically across time buckets.

**Stability**
The degree to which ratings remain consistent over time, absent changes in true skill. Measured by the `stability` metric (variance of rating changes across consecutive matches). A stable system does not overreact to individual match outcomes.

---

## Anti-Cheat

**Smurf**
A player whose true skill is substantially higher than their visible rating. Not a boolean flag or player type — smurf status is the combination of high `true_skill`, low `initial_rating`, and few `games_played` at population generation. Detection systems must infer smurf status from observable behavior.

**Detection**
The process of identifying abnormal player behavior (smurfing, boosting, deranking) from observable data. Implemented as `DetectionSystem` instances (Lua scripts) that observe match results and evaluate per-player evidence. Detection never reads ground truth.

**Intervention**
An action recommended by the detection system in response to flagged behavior. Actions escalate along a ladder: Warning, Restriction (queue modification), MatchmakingIsolation, Ban (removal from population). The escalation policy is configured in the detection script.
